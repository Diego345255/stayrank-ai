Why a hotel's guest rating can differ from its star rating

A hotel's star rating is typically the property's own self-assigned class. Its guest rating, when real review data exists, is a completely different number from a different source - and the gap between the two can tell you more than either alone.

Two different facts, kept separate

Star ratingSelf-reported

The property's own declared class (e.g. 3, 4, or 5 star) - a category, not a guest-experience score.

Guest ratingReal, when available

A live, review-based rating out of 10 from the connected booking-data source, shown only when real review data exists for that hotel - never estimated or backfilled with a guess.

StayRank AI never blends these into one combined figure. A 5-star property with a middling guest rating, or a modest 3-star property with an excellent one, both stay visible as two honest, separate facts.

What the "Guest Favorite" badge actually requires

At least 20 real reviews and a real guest rating of 9 or higher out of 10 - computed strictly from real review volume and score. It's never shown for a curated or procedurally generated listing with no real review data behind it, and it's never based on StayRank AI's own computed match score, which measures something different entirely (fit to your search, not guest satisfaction).

Common questions

Does StayRank AI ever invent a guest rating when real data isn't available?

No - if no real review data exists for a hotel, no guest rating is shown for it. Only the star rating (when reported) appears.

Is the Guest Favorite badge the same as StayRank AI's own match score?

No - the match score measures fit to your specific search; the Guest Favorite badge is computed only from real, independent review volume and score, unrelated to how well a hotel matches what you asked for.
